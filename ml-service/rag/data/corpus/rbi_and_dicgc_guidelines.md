# RBI & DICGC Regulatory Guidelines on Deposit Insurance, Sovereign Bonds & Retail Financial Safety

*Official Sources:* Reserve Bank of India (RBI) (`https://www.rbi.org.in`) & Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC) (`https://www.dicgc.org.in`)  
*Regulatory Framework:* Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation Act, 1961 (as amended) & RBI Government Securities Regulations  
*Trust Tier:* Tier 1 - Official Central Bank & Statutory Insurance Bodies

## SECTION 1: DICGC DEPOSIT INSURANCE COVERAGE & CLAIMS FRAMEWORK

The Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC), a wholly owned subsidiary of the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), provides statutory deposit insurance to retail bank depositors in India under the DICGC Act, 1961.

### 1.1 Maximum Protection Limit & Calculation
- **Insurance Ceiling**: Every depositor in an insured bank is insured up to a maximum limit of **₹5,00,000 (Five Lakh Rupees)**.
- **Combined Principal & Interest**: The ₹5 Lakh insurance limit covers both the **principal amount and accrued interest** held by the depositor across all accounts in a bank as on the date of bank liquidation, license cancellation, or restructuring scheme.
- **Cost of Insurance**: The insurance premium is paid entirely by the insured commercial and cooperative banks (currently 12 paise per ₹100 deposits per annum). Depositors pay **zero fees** for DICGC insurance coverage.

### 1.2 Insured Institutions Scope
DICGC covers deposits held across all licensed banking institutions in India:
1. All Commercial Banks (including public sector banks, private sector banks, foreign bank branches operating in India).
2. Regional Rural Banks (RRBs) and Local Area Banks (LABs).
3. Small Finance Banks (SFBs) and Payment Banks (for deposit balances held).
4. All Urban Cooperative Banks (UCBs), State Cooperative Banks (StCBs), and District Central Cooperative Banks (DCCBs).

### 1.3 Capacity and Right Rules (Same Right vs Different Capacity)
Deposit insurance is evaluated based on ownership capacity. Accounts held in the *same capacity and same right* in the same bank are aggregated under a single ₹5 Lakh ceiling, whereas accounts held in *different capacities* each receive separate ₹5 Lakh coverage.

#### Illustrative Capacities Entitled to Separate ₹5 Lakh Coverage per Bank:
1. **Single / Individual Capacity**: Accounts owned by an individual in their single name (e.g. John Doe - Savings + FD = Max ₹5 Lakhs).
2. **Joint Accounts**: Accounts held jointly by two or more individuals.
   - *Order of Names*: Joint accounts with different order of names (e.g. "John & Mary" vs "Mary & John") are legally treated as separate capacities, each eligible for up to ₹5 Lakhs cover.
3. **Partner of a Partnership Firm**: Account opened in the name of a registered partnership firm.
4. **Sole Proprietorship Account**: Account held for a business enterprise as a sole proprietor.
5. **Guardian for a Minor**: Account held by an individual as a legal guardian on behalf of a minor child.
6. **Trustee of an Explicit Trust**: Accounts managed by a trustee for specified beneficiaries.

### 1.4 Explicit Exclusions from DICGC Insurance Coverage
The following categories of deposits and financial products are **strictly excluded** from DICGC deposit insurance protection:
- Deposits of foreign Governments.
- Deposits of Central Government and State Governments.
- Inter-bank deposits (deposits held by one bank with another bank).
- Deposits held with Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) or housing finance companies (HFCs).
- Mutual fund units (Equity, Debt, Hybrid, Liquid funds).
- Corporate fixed deposits, non-convertible debentures (NCDs), and commercial paper.
- Direct equity stocks, derivative contracts, and commodity holdings.
- Government Securities, RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds, and Sovereign Gold Bonds.

## SECTION 2: RBI FLOATING RATE SAVINGS BONDS, 2020 (TAXABLE)

RBI Floating Rate Savings Bonds (FRSB 2020 Taxable) are sovereign debt instruments issued by the Reserve Bank of India on behalf of the Government of India under Section 3(vi) of the Government Securities Act, 2006.

### 2.1 Key Operational Specifications
- **Issuer & Safety**: Issued by Government of India (Sovereign Guarantee; absolute credit safety with zero default risk).
- **Tenure**: Fixed tenure of **7 years** from the date of issuance.
- **Minimum & Maximum Subscription**: Minimum investment of **₹1,000** (and in multiples of ₹1,000 thereafter). There is **no upper monetary ceiling** on maximum investment.
- **Interest Rate Linkage Formula**:
  - The interest rate is coupon-linked to the benchmark rate of the **National Savings Certificate (NSC)**.
  - Floating Rate Formula: **Interest Rate = NSC Rate + 0.35% (35 basis points)**.
  - *Example*: If the NSC rate is 7.70%, the FRSB 2020 interest rate is automatically set to 8.05% p.a.
- **Interest Reset Schedule**: Interest rate is reset semi-annually twice a year on **January 1** and **July 1**.
- **Payout Frequency**: Interest is paid out semi-annually on January 1 and July 1 directly to the investor's bank account. Cumulative option is not offered.

### 2.2 Tax Treatment & Secondary Market Liquidity
- **Income Taxability**: Interest income earned from FRSB 2020 is **100% taxable** at the investor's applicable income tax slab rate under the Income Tax Act, 1961.
- **Tax Deducted at Source (TDS)**: TDS is deducted at source at 10% on interest payouts if total annual interest exceeds ₹10,000 (unless Form 15G/15H is submitted).
- **Non-Transferability & Non-Tradability**: FRSB 2020 bonds are non-transferable, non-tradable in stock exchanges, and cannot be pledged as collateral for taking loans from banks/financial institutions.
- **Premature Redemption Window**: Premature encashment is permitted only for senior citizens after specified lock-in periods:
  - Investors aged 60 to 70 years: Lock-in period of 6 years (eligible after 6th year).
  - Investors aged 70 to 80 years: Lock-in period of 5 years.
  - Investors aged 80 years and above: Lock-in period of 4 years.
  - Penalty of 50% of the interest payable for the last six months applies on premature redemption.

## SECTION 3: SOVEREIGN GOLD BONDS (SGB) SCHEME FRAMEWORK

Sovereign Gold Bonds are government securities denominated in grams of gold, issued periodically by the RBI on behalf of the Government of India.

### 3.1 Key Specifications & Investor Benefits
- **Denomination & Investment Limits**: Denominated in units of 1 gram of gold. Minimum investment is 1 gram. Maximum annual subscription limit is **4 kg for individuals and HUFs**, and 20 kg for trusts per financial year.
- **Tenure**: Fixed tenure of **8 years**. Exit options are available after 5 years, exercisable on semi-annual interest payment dates.
- **Fixed Interest Payout**: Assured annual interest rate of **2.50% per annum** on the initial nominal investment amount, paid semi-annually.
- **Collateral Eligibility**: SGBs are eligible to be pledged as collateral for bank loans and traded on stock exchanges (NSE/BSE).

### 3.2 Tax Exemption & Capital Gains Rules
- **Tax on Annual Interest**: The 2.50% p.a. interest income is taxable at the investor's applicable slab rate.
- **Capital Gains Exemption on Redemption**: Under Section 47(viib) of the Income Tax Act, capital gains arising from the redemption of SGB at maturity (after 8 years) by an individual are **100% exempt from capital gains tax**.
- **Secondary Market Transfer**: Indexation benefits are available if SGB units are transferred/sold prior to maturity after holding for more than 24 months (LTCG taxed at 12.5%).
