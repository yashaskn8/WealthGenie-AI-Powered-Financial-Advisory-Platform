# Income Tax Department Regulations & Statutory Provisions (FY 2025-26 / AY 2026-27)

*Official Source:* Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT), Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance, Government of India (`https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in`)  
*Effective Assessment Year:* AY 2026-27 (Financial Year 2025-26)  
*Trust Tier:* Tier 1 - Official Government Body

## SECTION 1: NEW TAX REGIME (SECTION 115BAC — DEFAULT TAX REGIME)

### 1.1 Legislative Framework & Default Status
Under Section 115BAC of the Income Tax Act, 1961 (as amended by Finance Act 2024 for FY 2025-26), the New Tax Regime is the mandatory default tax regime for individuals, Hindu Undivided Families (HUFs), Association of Persons (AOPs), and Body of Individuals (BOIs). Taxpayers who wish to compute tax liability under the Old Tax Regime must explicitly opt out of Section 115BAC at the time of filing their Income Tax Return (ITR) under Section 139(1).

### 1.2 Income Tax Slabs & Rate Structure for FY 2025-26
For Assessment Year 2026-27 (Financial Year 2025-26), the tax slabs and applicable marginal tax rates under Section 115BAC are as follows:

| Net Taxable Income Range (₹) | Applicable Tax Rate | Cumulative Tax at Upper Threshold (₹) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | NIL (0%) | ₹0 |
| ₹4,00,001 to ₹8,00,000 | 5% | ₹20,000 |
| ₹8,00,001 to ₹12,00,000 | 10% | ₹60,000 |
| ₹12,00,001 to ₹16,00,000 | 15% | ₹1,20,000 |
| ₹16,00,001 to ₹20,00,000 | 20% | ₹2,00,000 |
| ₹20,00,001 to ₹24,00,000 | 25% | ₹3,00,000 |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | 30% | ₹3,00,000 + 30% on excess over ₹24 Lakhs |

### 1.3 Standard Deduction & Transport Allowance
- **Standard Deduction for Salaried Employees**: A flat deduction of **₹75,000** is allowed against gross salary income under Section 16(ia) for taxpayers assessed under the New Tax Regime.
- **Family Pension Standard Deduction**: Family pensioners under Section 57(iia) receive a standard deduction of ₹25,000 or 33.33% of pension, whichever is lower.
- **Employer NPS Contribution**: Employer contributions to an employee's NPS account under Section 80CCD(2) are allowed up to 14% of Salary (Basic + DA) for Central/State Government employees and 14% for private sector employees under the New Tax Regime.

### 1.4 Tax Rebate & Marginal Relief under Section 87A
- **Threshold for Full Tax Rebate**: Resident individual taxpayers whose net taxable income does not exceed **₹12,00,000** are entitled to a full tax rebate under Section 87A. The maximum rebate allowed under Section 87A for FY 2025-26 is **₹60,000**, rendering the net tax liability to ZERO for total income up to ₹12 Lakhs.
- **Marginal Relief Mechanism**: For resident individuals whose net taxable income marginally exceeds ₹12,00,000 (e.g. ₹12,10,000), marginal relief ensures that the total tax payable cannot exceed the amount of income in excess of ₹12,00,000.
  - *Formula*: Tax Payable = min(Standard Computed Tax, Net Income - ₹12,00,000).

## SECTION 2: OLD TAX REGIME (OPTIONAL OPT-OUT STRUCTURE)

### 2.1 Tax Slabs & Thresholds
Taxpayers opting out of Section 115BAC are taxed according to the traditional slab structure:

#### Individual Taxpayers (Age < 60 Years)
- **Up to ₹2,50,000**: NIL (0%)
- **₹2,50,001 to ₹5,00,000**: 5%
- **₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000**: 20%
- **Above ₹10,00,000**: 30%

#### Senior Citizens (Age 60 to 79 Years)
- **Up to ₹3,00,000**: NIL (0%)
- **₹3,00,001 to ₹5,00,000**: 5%
- **₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000**: 20%
- **Above ₹10,00,000**: 30%

#### Super Senior Citizens (Age 80 Years and Above)
- **Up to ₹5,00,000**: NIL (0%)
- **₹5,00,001 to ₹10,00,000**: 20%
- **Above ₹10,00,000**: 30%

- **Standard Deduction**: ₹50,000 for salaried employees under the Old Tax Regime.
- **Section 87A Rebate under Old Regime**: Max rebate of ₹12,500 for taxable income up to ₹5,00,000.

## SECTION 3: CHAPTER VI-A DEDUCTIONS & EXEMPTIONS (OLD REGIME)

### 3.1 Section 80C — Investments and Specified Outlays
The aggregate deduction permissible under Section 80C, Section 80CCC, and Section 80CCD(1) is subject to an overall monetary ceiling of **₹1,50,000** per financial year.

Eligible investments and expenses under Section 80C include:
1. **Equity Linked Savings Schemes (ELSS)**: Open-ended equity mutual funds with a statutory 3-year lock-in period. Highest historical return potential among 80C instruments.
2. **Public Provident Fund (PPF)**: 15-year scheme backed by Government of India. Interest earned and maturity proceeds are 100% tax-free (EEE status). Max annual deposit ₹1.5 Lakhs.
3. **Employees Provident Fund (EPF) & Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF)**: Employee contribution to EPF. Interest exempt up to ₹2.5 Lakhs employee contribution per year.
4. **Housing Loan Principal Repayment**: Principal portion of Equated Monthly Installments (EMI) paid on housing loan for a self-occupied or rented residential house property.
5. **Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)**: Deposit scheme for girl child aged below 10 years. EEE tax status with high interest rate.
6. **National Savings Certificates (NSC)**: 5-year post office fixed deposit scheme with compounded interest.
7. **Life Insurance Premiums**: Premiums paid for self, spouse, and children (premium should not exceed 10% of sum assured for policies issued after April 1, 2012).
8. **Tuition Fees**: Full-time education tuition fees paid for up to 2 children in Indian schools, colleges, or universities.
9. **5-Year Tax Saver Bank Fixed Deposits**: Fixed deposits with scheduled commercial banks having a mandatory lock-in period of 5 years.

### 3.2 Section 80D — Health Insurance Premiums & Medical Expenses
Section 80D permits deductions for health insurance premiums, critical illness riders, top-up policies, and preventive health check-ups:

- **Self, Spouse, and Dependent Children**:
  - Maximum deduction limit of **₹25,000** per financial year.
  - If self or spouse is a senior citizen (aged 60 years or older), the limit increases to **₹50,000**.
- **Parents**:
  - Additional deduction up to **₹25,000** for insurance premiums paid for parents below 60 years of age.
  - If parents are senior citizens (aged 60+), the limit increases to **₹50,000**.
- **Preventive Health Check-Up Sub-Limit**:
  - Up to **₹5,00,00** (₹5,000) within the overall applicable 80D ceiling for self, family, and parents.
- **Medical Expenditure for Senior Citizens without Insurance**:
  - Deduction up to ₹50,000 allowed for medical expenses incurred on senior citizen self/parents who are not covered under any health insurance policy.

### 3.3 Section 80CCD(1B) — National Pension System (NPS) Voluntary Contribution
- **Exclusive Additional Deduction**: Taxpayers can claim an additional deduction of up to **₹50,000** under Section 80CCD(1B) for voluntary contributions made to the NPS Tier-I account.
- **Over and Above Section 80C**: This ₹50,000 deduction is strictly over and above the ₹1,50,000 limit specified under Section 80C. Combining 80C and 80CCD(1B) yields a total deduction of **₹2,00,000**.
- **NPS Tax Status**: Upon reaching superannuation (age 60), 60% of the accumulated NPS corpus can be withdrawn as a lump sum completely tax-free. The remaining 40% must be used to purchase an annuity, which yields taxable pension income.

### 3.4 Section 10(13A) — House Rent Allowance (HRA) Exemption
Salaried individual taxpayers receiving HRA from their employer and residing in rented accommodation can claim tax exemption under Section 10(13A) read with Rule 2A.

The exempted HRA amount is the **minimum of the following three calculations**:
1. **Actual HRA received** from employer during the financial year.
2. **Rent paid minus 10% of Salary** (Salary = Basic Salary + Dearness Allowance forming part of retirement benefits).
3. **50% of Salary** if residing in metro cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai) OR **40% of Salary** if residing in non-metro locations.

- **Mandatory Documentation**: Permanent Account Number (PAN) of the landlord must be reported to the employer if total rent paid during the financial year exceeds ₹1,00,000.

## SECTION 4: CAPITAL GAINS TAXATION RULES (POST-FINANCE ACT 2024)

Capital gains taxes apply to profits realized from the transfer of capital assets.

### 4.1 Listed Equity Shares and Equity-Oriented Mutual Fund Schemes
For listed equity shares and equity mutual funds where Securities Transaction Tax (STT) is paid on acquisition and transfer:

- **Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG)** (Holding Period <= 12 Months):
  - Taxed at a flat rate of **20%** under Section 111A.
- **Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG)** (Holding Period > 12 Months):
  - Taxed at a flat rate of **12.5%** under Section 112A.
  - **Annual Exemption Threshold**: Aggregate long-term capital gains up to **₹1,25,000** in a financial year are completely exempt from tax. LTCG tax at 12.5% applies only to the net gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakhs.
  - Indexation benefit is not available for listed equity LTCG under Section 112A.

### 4.2 Debt Mutual Fund Schemes & Specified Mutual Funds
- **Post-April 1, 2023 Acquisition Rules**: Under Section 50AA, gains realized from mutual fund schemes where equity investment in domestic companies does not exceed 35% (e.g. Liquid funds, Short Duration debt funds, Corporate Bond funds, Conservative Hybrid funds) are deemed to be short-term capital gains regardless of the holding period.
- **Taxation Rate**: Capital gains on debt funds are added to the taxpayer's total income and taxed at the applicable marginal income tax slab rate (up to 30%).

### 4.3 Immovable Property (Land and Residential Buildings)
- **Classification Threshold**: Holding period of > 24 months qualifies as a Long-Term Capital Asset.
- **LTCG Rate**: Taxed at **12.5%** without indexation benefits under Section 112.
- **Grandfathering Provision for Pre-July 23, 2024 Property**: For land or building acquired prior to July 23, 2024, resident individuals and HUFs can compute LTCG tax under two options and pay whichever is lower:
  1. 12.5% tax rate without indexation.
  2. 20% tax rate with indexation benefit (using Cost Inflation Index [CII]).

### 4.4 Gold, Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB), and Unlisted Securities
- **Physical Gold and Gold ETFs**: Holding period > 24 months is long-term. LTCG is taxed at **12.5%** without indexation.
- **Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGB)**:
  - **Maturity Exemption**: Capital gains arising from redemption of SGB at maturity (after 8 years) by an individual taxpayer are **100% exempt from capital gains tax** under Section 47(viib).
  - **Premature Redemption / Sale**: Capital gains on transfer of SGB in secondary market before maturity are taxable (LTCG at 12.5% if held > 24 months).

## SECTION 5: HEALTH & EDUCATION CESS, SURCHARGE & MARGINAL RELIEF

### 5.1 Health and Education Cess
A mandatory Health and Education Cess of **4%** is levied on the total aggregate of income tax and surcharge across all income levels and both tax regimes.

### 5.2 Surcharge Rates for High Net Worth Individuals (HNIs)
Surcharge is levied on income tax payable when total income exceeds specified thresholds:

- **Income ₹50 Lakhs to ₹1 Crore**: 10% surcharge on income tax.
- **Income ₹1 Crore to ₹2 Crores**: 15% surcharge on income tax.
- **Income ₹2 Crores to ₹5 Crores**: 25% surcharge under Old Regime; capped at 25% under New Tax Regime.
- **Income above ₹5 Crores**: 37% surcharge under Old Regime; capped at **25%** maximum surcharge under the New Tax Regime (Section 115BAC).
- **Capped Surcharge on Capital Gains**: Surcharge on LTCG/STCG from listed equity shares (Sections 111A, 112A) is capped at a maximum rate of 15%.
